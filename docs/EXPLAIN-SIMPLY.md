# ARAH — the simple explanation

One page. For explaining to a client, a teacher, or a parent who does not want
the technical version.

---

## Say this

> "Imagine you could ask 207 students who already finished SPM:
> *what did you choose, and did it work out?*
>
> ARAH asks a new student the same ten questions those 207 answered. Then it
> finds the ones who answered most like them, and shows what **they** actually
> studied.
>
> It's not advice. It's what really happened to people like you."

That is the whole system. Everything else is detail.

---

## If they ask "how does it work?"

Four steps. Use these words:

**1. Ask** — Ten questions. Results, favourite subjects, how they like to
work. About three minutes.

**2. Match** — Compare those answers against 207 real SPM leavers.

**3. Count** — See which fields the most similar students actually went into.

**4. Show** — List the fields in order, and say how many students each one is
based on.

---

## The three numbers to remember

| Question | Answer |
| --- | --- |
| How many students is it based on? | **207 real Malaysian SPM leavers** |
| How often is it right? | **7 out of 10** — the student's real field is in the top three |
| Compared to just guessing? | Guessing gets about **5 out of 10** |

Say **7 out of 10**, not 71.5%. People trust a number they can picture.

---

## Three things to say before they ask

**"It can be wrong."**
About 3 in 10 students won't see their field in the top three. We show that
number on the website. It's a shortlist to think about, not a decision.

**"It doesn't guess."**
If a student hasn't picked a pre-university route yet, the system doesn't
invent one. It works out the answer for every route and blends them — and
tells the student it did that.

**"It won't make up numbers."**
If only nine students in the data chose a field, the system says so and
refuses to show statistics — because with that few people, someone could be
identified. It would rather show nothing than something misleading.

---

## The sentence that sells it

> **"It says *students like you chose this*.
> It never says *you should study this*."**

That is the difference between ARAH and a personality quiz. A school can put
its name next to it.

---

## Questions you'll get

**"Is it AI?"**
Yes — it learned patterns from real data instead of following rules someone
typed in. But it can't invent an answer. It can only report what's in the 207
records.

**"Why only 207?"**
That's how many real, complete responses exist. The system already has a way
to collect more: any former student can add their own outcome, an admin
approves it, and it joins the data.

**"Can't you download more data online?"**
Not honest data. National statistics only say *how many* people studied each
field — nothing about what those people were like, so there's nothing to learn
from. And the ready-made datasets online are mostly computer-generated. Adding
fake students would make the numbers look better and mean nothing.

**"Is student data safe?"**
A student's answers are visible only to them and the administrator, and that's
enforced by the database itself, not just hidden in the app. Students can
delete everything at any time.

> "A student answers ten questions.
> We compare them to 207 real SPM leavers.
> We show what the most similar students actually studied.
> It's right about 7 times out of 10, and it tells you when it isn't sure."
